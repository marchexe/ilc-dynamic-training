1 Summary

Objective. Does Population Based Training (PBT), which adapts the learning rate (LR) while fine-tuning the pretrained Particle Transformer (ParT), give a lower mistag than the standard ParT training recipe with the same budget of five models and 50 epochs? Figure 1 shows the whole study; the four findings below it link to the phases of the timeline.

Baseline correctedSep–Nov · Training (800k) and (8M) · one seed per grid The early PBT advantage was measured against a constant-LR baseline that was not tuned. In September PBT was 1.4% better (Training 800k) and 7.7% better (Training 8M) than constant-LR grids whose best LR was the highest one tried. After the grid was moved up to about 10−4, PBT was 0.5–1.0% better on one seed and 0.47% worse on a second seed with a different grid. Subsequent experiments did not establish a consistent advantage over tuned constant-LR training. Experimental setup and metric definitions

Training samples. The names give source entries, not jets. Training (800k) holds the first 800 thousand source entries of each of the three flavour files (b, c, light): 2,392,232 jets after preselection. Training (8M) holds the first 8 million entries of each file: 23,921,449 jets. Training (800k) is a subset of Training (8M). From 10 November the last 333,333 rows of each Training (8M) file form Validation (1M) and are not trained on, so Training (8M) then has 22,921,450 training jets, about ten times Training (800k).

Validation and Test. Validation (150k, until 9 November) and Validation (1M, from 10 November) select the model and drive PBT. Test (2.8M), 2,825,125 jets, is evaluated once per selected model. Test (9.0M), 8,970,477 jets, was used only in #10.

Metric. The mistag is in % (0.3018 means 0.3018%): the geometric mean of the background mistag rates at b-tag 80% and 90% (against c and light jets) and c-tag 50% and 80% (against b and light jets). Lower is better. “1.4% better” is a relative change of the mistag. Absolute values of Training (800k) and Training (8M) models differ by about a factor two and are not compared with each other.

Compute budget. Each compared method trains five models for 50 epochs from the same pretrained checkpoint: one model per LR (constant-LR grid), one per start LR (standard ParT), or a population of five (PBT). Exceptions: #2 and #4 train to 100 epochs, #2 by continuing two models of #1; #24 and #33 each add one standard ParT model, so the Training (8M) reference is the best of six models while PBT has five; the probe runs #25 and #26 train 12% and 9% more; in #28 each new seed trains only the two standard ParT models of the ensemble, which needs two forward passes per jet. GPU time of every run is in the experiment log.

Uncertainty. Intervals are 95%, from a block bootstrap of the Test sample. They do not include seed-to-seed variation, which the seeds show directly. Results with one seed (phase 2 grids, all Training (8M) PBT runs) have no estimate of seed-to-seed variation.

Use of the Test sample. Test (2.8M) was evaluated in 15 campaigns between 12 October and 25 November, and its results guided which experiment came next. Each comparison was fixed before its own Test evaluation, but Test is not an untouched sample: the PBT changes of #25–#26 and the ensemble of #27 were developed on seed 12345, whose Test results were known. The new seeds of #18 (32345) and #28 (22345, 32345) were trained after their comparisons were fixed. The intervals do not cover this repeated use.

Reading the timeline. Each phase lists its primary experiments; diagnostic checks and secondary modifications are under “Supporting experiments” and open from any link to them. # is the trial number; it links to the trial's runs in the experiment log. Dates are JST.

3 Conclusions and unresolved questions

Within the tested configurations, PBT improved on standard ParT training by less than 1% with Training (800k) and was worse with Training (8M); it showed no consistent advantage over a tuned constant LR. The only large gain found with Training (8M) came from averaging two separately trained standard ParT models.

  • Observations and hypotheses. That the model overfits with Training (800k) and not with Training (8M), and that PBT lowered its LRs early, are observations. That they explain why PBT helps only with Training (800k), and why pairs of similar quality gain from averaging, are hypotheses, not tested directly.
  • Seeds. All Training (8M) PBT results and every phase-2 grid rest on one seed. The effect of the decision frequency (#6) is inconclusive; decisions within an epoch (#11) were never run.
  • Test reuse. Test (2.8M) guided the order of experiments (15 evaluation campaigns); only the new seeds of #18 and #28 are clean replications.
  • Scope. One pretrained checkpoint, one optimizer, 50 epochs. No result was carried into a physics analysis.
